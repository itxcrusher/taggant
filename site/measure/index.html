Measure a camera

Print the postcard artwork, measure how wide it came out, and point this at it. It reports how much of the world your camera's picture covers, which is the one number the print readiness report assumes rather than measures. Hold it close and square on: the artwork has to fill most of the picture before it can be recognised at all, so start at about 150 mm from a 148 mm wide copy and back off until it is lost.

On paper, not on a screen. A screen's own pixel grid beats against the camera's and takes recognition apart: against a clean print the matcher agreed on 102 points, and against a grid at the period that beats worst, 12. It still finds the artwork, in flickers, which is worse than not finding it.

Hold it square on, and turning sideways matters more than tipping. Tipping shortens one horizontal edge and stretches the other, which cancels in the width this reads; turning shortens both together and nothing cancels. Past about fifteen degrees the reading says TILTED and will not record, because a tilted reading is wrong rather than merely noisy. The figure is measured across the picture's width, so it depends on which way up the phone is: the camera line says which, and a phone held upright gives the narrower of its two fields.
